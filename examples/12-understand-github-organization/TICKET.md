# Recognize Repository Ownership and Find Repos in an Organization

## 1. Goal

Build the owner instinct: when you see a repository you know who it belongs to, when you cannot find one you know where to look, and when you create one you know whose name it will land under. This lesson asks you to create or change nothing. Opening things and taking a look is enough.

---

## 2. What to Do

1. Read this lesson's README and get clear that a repo's full coordinate is owner plus name, that is `github.com/OWNER/REPO`.
2. Open one of your own repos and one well known open source project, read the owner out of each address bar, and click into the owner's page to see whether it is a personal account or an organization.
3. Click your avatar in the top right, choose Organizations, and see which organizations you have joined. Having none is normal; just remember the entry point.
4. If you have any, open one of them, click the Repositories tab at the top, and search for a keyword to get a feel for searching rather than scrolling.
5. Get one thing straight: being able to open a repository does not mean you joined that organization, since you may only be a collaborator on that one repo.
6. In GitHub Desktop, use File, New Repository to create any local repo, click Publish repository, expand the Organization dropdown to see the options, then close it without actually publishing.

**Estimated time:** About 15 minutes

---

## 3. Checklist

- [ ] **Understand the coordinate**: you can explain that a repository name alone is not unique, that owner plus name is, and you can read the owner off a URL.
- [ ] **Understand Organization**: you can explain in your own words that an organization is an account that can own repositories, and how it relates to a personal account.
- [ ] **Know your list holds only yours**: you can explain that your personal Repositories list only shows repositories under your own name, and that repositories shared with you are not there.
- [ ] **Can check your organizations**: you found the Organizations entry in the avatar menu and know which organizations you currently belong to (or confirmed you belong to none).
- [ ] **Can find repos in an organization**: you know to open an organization's Repositories tab and use the search box, and that the address is `github.com/orgs/ORG_NAME/repositories`.
- [ ] **Can tell access from membership**: you can explain that opening a repository does not mean you joined that organization, and that the full URL is then your only reliable way in.
- [ ] **Have seen the owner choice**: you have seen the Organization dropdown in GitHub Desktop's Publish repository window and know that None means publishing under your own name.
