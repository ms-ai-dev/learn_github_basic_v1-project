# Understand Collaborators, and Keep This String Taut

## 1. Goal

Understand what a collaborator is, be able to walk through every step of adding one, and lodge the string "no invite, no collaboration" firmly in your head. This lesson does not force you to find someone to practice with right now; the focus is on understanding clearly, so that on the day a real collaboration need shows up (an advisor, a classmate, or a student you mentor wanting into your repo), you know what to do.

---

## 2. What to Do

1. Read this lesson's README and understand what a collaborator is, and that in a personal repo they have both Read and Write by default, meaning they can both view and edit.
2. Remember the add path: repo, Settings, Collaborators under Access on the left, Add people.
3. Get clear that an invite recognizes the other person's exact GitHub username, not a display name and not an email; and understand that to get invited you need to actively give others your own exact username or profile link.
4. Get clear that an invite takes effect only when the other person accepts; before they accept, the status is Pending invite.
5. If you happen to have a willing classmate or a second account of your own on hand, you can actually send an invite following section 8 of the README (optional).
6. Remember one conclusion: once you settle on a username, do not keep changing it; the specific reasons will be covered separately later.

**Estimated time:** About 15 minutes

---

## 3. Checklist

- [ ] **Understand Collaborator**: you can explain what a collaborator is, and that in a personal repo they have Read plus Write permissions by default.
- [ ] **Remember the add path**: you can name the path repo, Settings, Collaborators under Access, Add people.
- [ ] **Use the username correctly**: you know an invite uses the exact username rather than the email, and you know to clearly give your own username or profile link to whoever wants to invite you.
- [ ] **Understand the accept flow**: you can explain that the other person must accept the invite before they officially become a collaborator, and that before they accept it is Pending invite.
- [ ] **Remember the bottom line**: you can explain that for a private repo, without an invite, others cannot access it and cannot collaborate.
- [ ] **(Optional) Do it once**: if you can, actually invite a person and see the Pending invite in the list.
- [ ] **Know not to change the username around**: remember not to keep changing the username once settled, with the reasons to be covered separately later.
